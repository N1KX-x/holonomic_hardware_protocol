Nominal Velocity Model of the Unitree Go2

October 2026

1  General Model

Each command segment \(k\) holds a forward speed \(v_x \ge 0\) and a yaw rate \(\omega\) for a duration \(T\). The yaw command is split into its left and right parts, \(\omega^{+} = \max(\omega, 0)\) and \(\omega^{-} = \min(\omega, 0)\). The body-frame velocity the robot achieves is modelled as

$$\begin{bmatrix} \tilde v_x \\ \tilde\omega^{+} \\ \tilde\omega^{-} \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} g_x & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & g_\omega^{+} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & g_\omega^{-} \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{G}_3} \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ \omega^{+} \\ \omega^{-} \end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{T}\begin{bmatrix} o_x\,\mathbf{1}(v_x>0) \\ o_\omega^{+}\,\mathbf{1}(\omega>0) \\ o_\omega^{-}\,\mathbf{1}(\omega<0) \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \kappa\, v_x \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \tag{1}$$

and the yaw rate of the robot is \(\tilde\omega = \tilde\omega^{+} + \tilde\omega^{-}\). Here \(g_x\) and \(o_x\) are the forward gain and offset, \(g_\omega^{\pm}\) and \(o_\omega^{\pm}\) the left (\(+\)) and right (\(-\)) turn gains and offsets, \(\mathbf{1}(\cdot)\) equals 1 when its condition holds and 0 otherwise, and \(\kappa\) is the yaw drift per metre of commanded forward walking.

2  Turning from Standstill and after Running

The robot needs time to begin a turn, and this time depends on whether its gait is already running. We therefore distinguish the case \(c_k\) of every in-place turn segment (\(v_x = 0,\ \omega \neq 0\)):

$$c_k = \begin{cases} \mathrm{st} & \text{first segment of a turn, robot at standstill } (k = 0 \text{ or } \mathbf{u}_{k-1} = \mathbf{0}),\\ \mathrm{run} & \text{first segment of a turn, right after running } (\mathbf{u}_{k-1} \neq \mathbf{0}),\\ \mathrm{cont} & \text{later segment of the same turn}. \end{cases} \tag{2}$$

The turn gains and offsets in (1) take the values of that case:

$$g_\omega^{\pm} = g_{\omega,c_k}^{\pm}, \qquad o_\omega^{\pm} = o_{\omega,c_k}^{\pm}, \qquad c_k \in \{\mathrm{st}, \mathrm{run}, \mathrm{cont}\}. \tag{3}$$

A turn that starts from standstill loses about \(\lambda_{\mathrm{st}} = 0.30\) s of its first segment and one that starts right after running about \(\lambda_{\mathrm{run}} = 0.10\) s. Losing this time at the steady turn rate is equivalent to a smaller gain in the first segment,

$$g_{\omega,c}^{\pm} = g_{\omega,\mathrm{cont}}^{\pm}\left(1 - \frac{\lambda_c}{T}\right), \qquad c \in \{\mathrm{st}, \mathrm{run}\}. \tag{4}$$
